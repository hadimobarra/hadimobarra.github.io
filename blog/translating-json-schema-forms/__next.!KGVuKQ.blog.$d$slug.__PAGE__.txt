10:T1110,<h2 id="text-that-isnt-in-your-code">Text that isn't in your code</h2>
<p>Data platforms connect to dozens of systems, and each has its own settings. Nobody writes those forms by hand. They're generated from <a href="https://json-schema.org/">JSON Schema</a>: a field's <code>title</code> becomes its label and <code>description</code> becomes its help text.</p>

<p>Then you localize the product. Your translation files cover every string in your components, and the connection form is still in English. The text comes from a schema, often written by a backend team, sometimes in another repository.</p>
<p>I worked on this while localizing an OpenMetadata-based platform into Persian. The code below is a simplified illustration of the approach, not the product's source.</p>
<h2 id="three-ways-that-dont-last">Three ways that don't last</h2>
<p><strong>Edit the schemas.</strong> They're generated or owned by someone else, and the next upgrade overwrites you.</p>
<p><strong>Translate in the form's templates.</strong> Looking up <code>t(label)</code> with the English text as the key works until two fields share a title. "Name" might need different words for a person and a database.</p>
<p><strong>Keep a copy of each schema per language.</strong> The copies drift. A new required field appears in English and not in Persian, and the form starts sending invalid data.</p>
<h2 id="translate-the-schema-not-the-form">Translate the schema, not the form</h2>
<p>The idea that holds up: treat a schema as data, pull its text out into a catalog, and put translated text back just before rendering. The form generator never knows.</p>
