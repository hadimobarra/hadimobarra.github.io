14:T96f,<h2 id="what-architecture-is-for">What architecture is for</h2>
<p>A good structure makes one thing cheap: changing your mind. When a requirement moves, how many files move with it? Two questions decide that more than any folder layout. Where does each piece of state live? And what may import what?</p>

<ul>
<li><strong>Local state</strong> for what only one component cares about: an open menu, an input's draft.</li>
<li><strong>Lifted state</strong> when siblings need it. Move it to the nearest common parent, no higher.</li>
<li><strong>The URL</strong> for anything a user would want to share or keep after a reload: filters, tabs, the selected row.</li>
<li><strong>A server cache</strong>, such as <a href="https://tanstack.com/query/latest">TanStack Query</a>, for data that belongs to the server. Copying it into your own store makes you responsible for its freshness.</li>
<li><strong>A global store</strong> for what is truly global and truly yours: the session, the theme, an unsaved draft across pages.</li>
</ul>
<p>The URL is the most underused step. Filters in the URL bring back, forward, bookmarks and shared links for free.</p>

<h3 id="validate-where-data-enters">Validate where data enters</h3>
<p>Types describe what you <em>hope</em> the server sends. Check it once, in <code>api.ts</code>, and the rest of the feature can rely on it. <a href="/blog/never-trust-json-parse-in-render/">Never trust JSON.parse in a render</a> shows what happens otherwise.</p>

<h2 id="container-and-presentational-components">Container and presentational components?</h2>
<p>Older guides, including an earlier version of this one, recommended splitting every component into a "container" and a "presentational" half. Hooks made that ceremony unnecessary: a custom hook separates the logic without a wrapper component. Its author <a href="https://medium.com/@dan_abramov/smart-and-dumb-components-7ca2f9a7c7d0">says the same</a>. Keep the idea, separate deciding from drawing, and drop the pairs of files.</p>
